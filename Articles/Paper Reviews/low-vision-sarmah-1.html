Predicting Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital in Low Vision Students

A Review

Josh Mastiff

Paper Title: Parental Involvement and Belongingness as Predictors of Psychological Capital and Psychological Well-Being Among Students with Low Vision

Authors: Dr. Monikankana Sarmah, Assistant Professor at the Department of Psychology, Assam Don Bosco University-ADBU (then research scholar at the Department of Psychology, Gauhati University)

(Retired)Dr. Indranee Phookan Borooah, Professor at the Department of Psychology, Gauhati University

Journal: Paripex - Indian Journal of Research

Date: June, 2023

Source:PARENTAL INVOLVEMENT AND BELONGINGNESS AS PREDICTORS OF PSYCHOLOGICAL CAPITAL AND PSYCHOLOGICAL WELL-BEING AMONG STUDENTS WITH LOW-VISION

Introduction

Sight.

The ability to see, to perceive the world visually is one of the most gratifying and profound aspects of being human. The precision that comes with a layout of the direct access to the space around that we navigate, the perplexity of shapes, patterns, geometries and the vibrance of colors; vision is profound and an indispensable cornerstone of human existence. The capability, architecture and evolution of the visual organ- the eye, and its adjacent neural processes and developments in the brain's visual cortex are fascinating. Our hominid ancestors would probably not have done as well without the visual accuracy we possess. While it can be speculated if such intelligent life could have evolved without highly specialized vision, one thing is clear. Our specific evolution has a lot to do with our sight and the advancement and delicacies of modern life are centered around and optimized for vision.

It does not mean a lack of vision renders a life completely lackluster, life and consciousness under any case are awestriking and serene. But the absence of vision is, generally, a profound perceptual lack. Ask any able-eyed person if they would trade sight for abundance and grandeur- chances are most would not only be hesitant but downright rejecting of such a proposal. Just close your eyes once and imagine the void of the eyelids forever closing in on the rich palette of colors and shapes you see. Its a scary thought to lose such detail. And yet, it is a reality for so many. Their existence is not sad or sorry; far from it. Their lives are as complex and radiant as any of ours. They can experience this dance of life, think in concretes and abstractions, be real, ugly, beautiful and everything in between just like any human being can be.

Yet, there is some sympathy to be had. Not only do such individuals miss out on the wonder of sight we take as a given at most times, in an ableist global society they face immeasurable practical problems. Unlike bats booned with ultrasound spatial navigation (alas we shall never know how they feel, I curse Mr. Nagel!), humans rely primarily on their sight to navigate, as our other sense organs not primed to be as spatially precise. The blind and the visually impaired face a whole sleuth of challenges able-eyed folks hardly think of. They may need lifelong assistance for locomotion, or their movements might be limited to the efficiency of aiding tools like assistance animals and canes. While brailles have been developed so such individuals can read, it is significantly less efficient than simply reading, takes much more time and not all facilities are equipped to cater to their needs. The lack of sight also means communication is disrupted as so much of interpersonal communication rely on overt gesticulations. Safety concerns are also a thing as during crises, the blind and visually impaired might not be equipped to navigate chaos and catastrophe and they have also been disproportionately affected by crimes, schemes and even wild encounters. While cases of extraordinary resilience and individuals heightening their other senses to lead lives with a semblance of normalcy do exist, they are few and far between. Impractical for most given the harsh landscape and practicalities of modern life.

Psychologists are naturally interested in the cognitive capacities as well as the functioning and well-being of the blind and visually impaired. This is exactly what Dr. Sarmah, professor at ADBU, Tapesia in the Department of Psychology (then research scholar at Gauhati University) and her supervisor (at the time) professor at the Department of Psychology at Gauhati University, Dr. Borooah, were interested in. The pair set out to understand how Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital of low vision students are impacted by factors such as parental involvement and belongingness. The study sought to find out how low vision students, facing significant life challenges, navigate their environments, particularly their academic environments at schools. Such students may face functional hinderances that make daily navigation through curricula and the school environment particularly rough. Also a sad part of life is having to deal with unempathetic people who ostracize and make fun of people for their disabilities. Kids at school can be particularly mean. But also most people unintentionally enable feelings of isolation and otherness among those with severe functional disabilities.

Terminology

Psychological Well-Being : It refers to the state of optimal mental health and flourishing, encompassing a positive sense of self, the ability to cope with life's challenges, and a sense of overall satisfaction and happiness. The Ryff's Psychological Well-Being model proposes 6 components contributing to overall Psychological Well-Being- Self-Acceptance, Positive Relations with Others, Autonomy, Environmental Mastery, Purpose in Life and Personal Growth. Factors like social connections and support, resilience and coping skills, positive mindset and gratitude influence Psychological Well-Being.

Psychological Capital : It refers to the positive psychological resources and strengths that individuals possess, enabling them to thrive, succeed, and cope effectively in various domains of life. It encompasses 4 key components- Self-Efficacy, Hope, Optimism and Resilience. In this specific paper, the concept of Academic Psychological Capital is significant, which refers to the positive psychological resources and strengths that contribute to academic success, well-being, and resilience among students.

Parental Involvement : The active role parents play in the lives of children that determines their development, emotional regulations, social relationships, etc. In this study, Parental Involvement refers to parents actively aiding students in their academic work like curricula and helping them navigate their academic environments through validation, participation and motivation.

Belongingness : It refers to the innate human need to be connected, accepted, and valued by others within a particular group, community, or social context and is crucial for an individual's Psychological Well-Being, self-esteem, and overall sense of identity. In the context of school belongingness, it is the feeling of being accepted, valued, and connected within the school community.

Low Vision : It is defined as not being blind but significantly visually impaired in ways that corrective glasses or lenses cannot compensate for due to factors such as cataract, glaucoma, macular degeneration, or congenital conditions.

Methodology

The study included a multi-stage sampling method within the Kamrup Metro district of Assam. 4 administrative blocks, 2 from the North zone and 2 from the South zone were randomly selected in the first stage and from those 22 government schools were selected using purposive sampling in the second stage, considering the availability of low vision students. The sample size was sufficiently large with 210 students, of which a 150 had normal vision and 60 had low vision. Furthermore, the sample was divided on gender grounds, with 75 boy and 75 girl students of which 30 from both groups were low vision students.

Data collection made use of 4 standardized psychological tools: the Perceived Parental Involvement Questionnaire (Raj & Sasikala, 2018). the General Belongingness Scale (Malone et al., 2012), the Academic Psychological Capital Questionnaire (Luthans et al., 2007), and the Ryff's Psychological Well-Being Scale (1984). The researcher and supervisor pair together worked to translate the tools from English to simple Assamese and adhering to ethical principles, administered them to the sample.

The data was analyzed using a statistical tool, the IBM SPSS Statistics 21 (now discontinued due to launch of newer versions), and found to be non linear so a non-parametric approach was taken. Accordingly the Mann Whitney U test was used to assess the difference in Parental Involvement, Belongingness, Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital based on gender and vision status. The Spearman Rank Correlation was used to determine the covariance between the aforementioned variables. And lastly, Multiple Regression Analysis was used to predict Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital from Parental Involvement and Belongingness.

Results

The Mann Whitney U didn't find significant discrepancies in gender based comparisons except in the case of Psychological Capital, where girls significantly possessed more Psychological Capital then boys, attributing it to gender based struggles women face in day to day life that can lead to stronger resilience. The Mann Whitney U however found significant differences across the comparison of all 4 variables, finding low vision students compared lower than normal students across Parental Involvement, Belongingness, Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital. Particularly striking was the Parental Involvement factor as parents of normal vision students were more involved academically with course work, etc., but parents of low vision students were louder advocates for adjustments and compensations for the visually impaired.

The Spearman Rank Correlation found significant positive correlations between both Parental Involvement and Belongingness with Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital, respectively, across both gender based and vision based analysis. Parental Involvement and Belongingness positively correlated with Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital between boys/girls and normal/low vision students.

The Multiple Regression Analysis showed both Parental Involvement and Belongingness predicted Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital, with Belongingness being a predictor of both Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital.

Going Forward

The study confirms that low vision students, on average, struggle with Psychological Well-Being, with feeling positive and happy, and Psychological Capital, likely having esteem issues and lower hope and optimism. It also showed such students struggle with feeling they belong to and are part the school culture. What's most striking is parents of such students are academically less involved and no reasons have been given for such an interesting finding. Low vision also indiscriminately affects both boys and girls across all discussed variables.

The research shows that low vision students can benefit from better accommodations and inclusivity in academic and school settings by educating their regular peers to treat them in accordance to their needs and make them feel welcomed. Parents of low vision students can also play their parts and have bigger roles in their academic journey by being better and more involved mentors, participate more in school events and be better advocates.

Critique

There was a missed statistical opportunity as the Spearman Rank Correlation predicted correlations between Parental Involvement and Belongingness with Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital based on gender and vision status but further analysis on if the correlation was stronger among a specific group wasn't undertaken. It would be interesting to know if the correlates were stronger between say Belongingness and Psychological Well-Being in boys vs girls.

Also an observation is that the paper spent significant time in descriptive and exploratory analysis with only prediction as a mention in the title, which was delivered in promptly in the 2 tables of Multiple Regression Analysis(Tables 4.17 and 4.18). Gender as a variable could've been mentioned given the valuable findings based on that factor.

Overall the publication was a little condensed but that is most probably due to journal adherence.

There are no problems with the study itself as it was pretty robust and does what it sought out to do. The criticisms are non structural but constructive, more about clarity of publication and refinement.

Conclusion

The paper thus highlighted is an important piece of literature, bringing attention to an underrepresented and struggling population- the visually impaired. The insights and implications are valuable. The study confirms intuitive assumptions- that Parental Involvement and Belongingness significantly impact Psychological Well-Being and Psychological Capital. More studies on the visually impaired are necessary and Dr. Sarmah and Dr. Borooah set a great precedent in the correct direction.

It also makes one ponder on the value of sight and makes us re-evaluate empathy. The blind, visually impaired and low vision folks struggle from things able-bodied people don't have to bother with. Treat them with respect for they're human and if possible, make accommodations.

And lets appreciate the beauty of every color and shape that helped you read this review. At least for a moment, lets revere to how spectacular vision truly is.
