NASA launches the Roman telescope

NASA launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, shortened in most cases to the Roman Space Telescope, named after NASA's first chief astromer, Nancy Grace Roman, on 30 August at 7:26AM EDT from the Kennedy Space Center aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket. The rocket used a 2.4 meter primary mirror, originally to be used for a spy satellite, a smaller secondary mirror and an experimental coronagraph to block the glare if starlight to directly image exoplanets. The Roman Space Telescope has a field view 100 times largerer than the Hubble Telescope. The telescope can image infrared light waves at a high resolution, just like the JWST, but the wider field view can allow it for more discovery. Looking into older stars can reveal important details about the rates of supernovae as scientists seek to study Type Ia supernovae and gravitaglobal lensing among other probes that can reveal the acceleration of the cosmic expansion and eventually dark matter and dark energy through other complementary measures.
