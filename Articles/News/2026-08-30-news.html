Air purifiers may aid in overall cognitive health

Researchers at the Department of Public Health Sciences, University of Connecticut Health Center, Farmington, CT, USA, looked at the cognitive benefits of indoor air purifiers and may have found potential benefits. Using a longitudinal method for 3 months, the study tracked 156 participants aged 30 and above to test their cognitive abilities after sustained periods of using high-efficiency particulate arrestance (HEPA) air purifiers. The participants selected lived in a 200m range near highways in Somerville, Massachusetts and were of sound reasoning. The study involved randomly splitting the participants into 2 groups. One groups was given a month to use HEPA purifiers, then a cooldown period of another month, then a placebo purifier that looked like the real HEPA purifier but did nothing, and the vice versa order for the other group. The participants were then given Trail Marking Tests of 2 parts, Part A and Part B, and participants were timed for sequentially connecting numbers (Part A) and sequentially connecting alternating numbers and letters (Part B). Part A of the Trail Making Test assesses visual memory and motor speed skills, while Part B assesses executive function and mental flexibility. Each participant was tested early morning of the beginning and end of each period so each participant had taken the test 4 times. The results were of 119 participants as the remaining participant data was incomplete.

The study found a 12% improvement in participants aged 40 and higher in the time taken for Part B of the Trail Making Tests after periods spent indoors with real HEPA purifiers, highlighting improvements in executive function and mental flexibility and the study controls for factors like time spent indoors and perceived stress. No such differences were observed in participants below 40. The time differences are also not significantly different but they are once age is moderated in participants at or above 40. The study attributes these to potential increase in white matter due to better air quality and the researchers aim to put their hypothesis to the test in subsequent studies. No significant differences were seen in Part A timings. The study also saw a learning effect as participants who had taken the test with the sham first and HEPA later all did significantly better at later tests. It is however a thing to question if taking the same test itself makes the task easier as that factor was not explicitly accounted for in the paper.

It is an interesting paper looking at a novel solution to a potential problem affecting people in this age of pollution. More studies need to be done to fully grasp cognitive developmental hazards due to air pollution and methods to circumvent these issues.

Source:

Effect of HEPA filtration air purifiers on cognitive function from a secondary outcome analysis of a pragmatic randomized crossover trial
