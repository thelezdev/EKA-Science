Multi dimensional problems may be leading to poor adaptation in plants

Researchers at the University of Michigan led by Sasha G D Bishop undertook a longitudinal study over 9 years that analyzed traits in the morning glory flower, ipomoea purpurea.

The study analyzed the evolutionary trajectory of the plant over the years and realized that multiple adaptation challenges may lead to prioritizations of certain adaptations that solve a specific problem while the development of the specific trait may result in the plant being worse off in facing other problems. The researchers identified the plants facing problems from both increasing climate change and a reduction in pollinators. The plant in response to these bottlenecks is favouring bigger flowers to attract more pollinators. However a better adaptation to climate change is to flower earlier and more frequently, suggesting competing problems may be exacerbating the survival problems of already vulnerable populations.

The rate of adaptation at the start of the study period was expected to be at 76%. However, by year 9, adaptation rates had starkly fallen by 96% in morning glory.

A fascinating study that is equal parts worrying as it is informative in analyzing the covariance of different problems that drive plant evolution.
