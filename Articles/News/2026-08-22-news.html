Quantum Computing may be Facing a Replication Crisis

In a recent publication making news headlines in various important science publications, like Young Scientist, an arXiv preprint by researchers from the Leibniz Universität Hannover (Hannover, Germany) lead by Dominik Köster claims that their research indicates reproducibility is not yet consistently achieved in quantum computing research. The researchers conducted a large-scale automated screening of nearly 5000 quantum computing papers and manually evaluated a curated sample of 127 papers, using a five-question framework that covers code availability, environment specification, documentation, hardware description, and executability. Their manual analysis reveals that only 24.4% of the sampled papers provide code artifacts, and among those, 64.5% fail to execute successfully in a clean environment and this assessment is corroborated by the large-scale automated analysis that yields a consistent code availability rate of 26.8%. Further, it shows that approximately one-third of the papers with accessible code lack machine-readable environment specifications.

This study comes in the light of an earlier collaborative paper this year, including researchers from the University of Pittsburg, University of Minnesota and the Institut Néel in France lead by Sergey M. Frolov that claimed that 'smoking gun' effects in topological quantum computing may be due to faulty practices like selection bias where the messy complete data is discarded and only specific data that can mimic the signals of true topological states are reported. The study, which took years to be published as replication papers are often labelled 'unoriginal', advices researchers to transparently publish full data and explore the complete parameter space where true topological states can occur and not blindly be guided by theory.

Sea urchin inspired bone implants developed by researchers at IIT Mandi

Researchers from IIT Mandi led by Dr. Sumit Murab have devised a novel bone implant that improves the bonding between implants and bones and can protect from bacteria commonly affecting contemporary bone implantation that may require pharmaceutical interventions or even surgery. They utilized 3D printed and biodegradable Polylactic Acid (PLA) as the base and treated it with an alkaline solution due to PLAs hydrophobic nature and make it an active site for mineral deposition. Then they subjected the alkalized PLA to hydrothermal treatment at 90°C that induced the growth of spiky clusters of hydroxyapatite nanospicules, as seen in sea urchins, that can mechanically ward off bacteria. The implant fosters better osteointegration that allows bone cells to better integrate with the biocompatible structure.

It is a significant achievement and can see much needed surgical adoption in the near future.

Warming Crisis in the Himalayas that Disproportionately Affects the Western Himalayas, affecting Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh and Himachal Pradesh and even the North East

A recent study led by Protyusha Mukhopadhyay and Swagata Payra from Birla Institute of Technology, Mesra, in collaboration with the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology and Ashoka University that analyzed 120 years of observed temperature data (1901–2020) and projected future trends using eight global climate models up to the year 2100 for the Eastern, Western and Southern ranges of the Himalayas. The study indicates that the Western Himalayas are warming in stark disproportion to the Eastern and Southern ranges. Spring temperatures in the Western Himalayas have already risen by 1.08°C, compared to only 0.83°C in both the Central and Eastern Himalayas and winter temperatures in the Western Himalayas could soar by 7.18°C by the end of the century (2081–2100). In comparison, the Eastern Himalayas are projected to warm by a "lower" 5.82°C. This means that by 2100, spring snow loss in the west could reach 95.9 kg per square meter under high emissions, potentially leading to an almost complete loss of seasonal snow in some pockets.

A concerning but very important study that unabashedly deserves the public eye.
