Dietary changes may lead to reduced biological aging in just 4 weeks

Researchers at the University of Sydney led by Dr. Caitlin Andrews have found dietary changes in fat, carbohydrates and animal proteins can significantly impact biological aging.

Biological aging looks at biological markers like blood cholesterol levels, epigenetic clocks, etc., that indicate certain physiological characteristics at the cellular level which are typical of some age group. The team combined information from 20 different biomarkers, including blood levels of cholesterol, insulin, and C-reactive protein. Together, these measures were used to calculate a biological age score for participants. 104 Australian adults aged 65-75 were randomized into 4 different groups: omnivorous high-fat (OHF), omnivorous high-carbohydrate (OHC), semi-vegetarian high-fat (VHF), or semi-vegetarian high-carbohydrate (VHC).

Significant effect on biomarkers were observed in 3 of the 4 groups, excluding only omnivorous high-fat diet (OHF). Statistically, the most significant group were those taking an omnivorous high-carbohydrate diet (OHC).

The study is preliminary and the research doesn't show if these effects last in the long term or if the reduced biological markers actually lead to longer lifespans.

It is however an interesting development and more work needs to be done.
