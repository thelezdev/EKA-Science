UN report confirms exceeding 1.5°C warming is now inevitable

In a concerning report published on 2 September, 2026, the UNEP (United Nations Environmental Programme) harrowingly admitted that under current policies and alternative near-term trajectories, exceedance of 1.5°C is now widely assessed as unavoidable.

The COP21 at the Paris Agreement established that a 2°C increase in temperature relative to pre-industrial periods would be critical and keeping temperatures well below that figure was the need of the hour, where the ambitious goal of keeping warming below 1.5°C was also iterated. This has been the backbone of all international climate policy. A breach in that number highlights a grim change.

The report identifies the critical 1.5°C threshold to be breached as early by 2030 and predicts a rise to 2.6°C if current trends persist. This will be devastating if it reaches 2.6°C, numerous studies have established the tipping point to be reached at 2°C that with stark warnings. The report says under the best case scenario, temperatures will peak at 1.8°C.

Reports say the globe will experience a temporary breach beyond 1.5°C, followed immediately by efforts to bring the temperature below 1.5°C. According to the report, there will be an overshoot above the 1.5°C mark, a peak to destabilizing and dangerous temperatures and a gradual decline, aiming to again reach below 1.5°C by 2100.

A critical report.
