Researchers achieve quantum entanglement with photons in sunlight

In a brilliant feat of collaborative applied physics between researchers from the University of Ottawa and the Max Planck Institute for the Science of Light (MPL) in Germany, a novel experimental design pushed the boundaries of experimentation by demonstrating quantum entanglement in light waves coming from sunlight. Quantum entanglement is usually achieved in coherent light like in lasers where the peaks and valleys follow predictable patterns. While quantum entanglement has been achieved in incoherent light like that in LEDs, work by the same team of researchers, it still isn't standard practice. Sunlight on the other hand is highly incoherent, having different wavelengths, amplitudes and directions all emitted at once, having high spatial and temporal incoherence. Researchers outside the experiments were rightfully skeptical if such entanglement could be detected from sunlight. To put the theoretical work of Robert Boyd's team from the University of Ottawa to the test, the team used a tiny non-linear crystal that produces quantum entangled particles using spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC), where a pump beam enters the non-linear crystal and individual photons may split off to create entangled pairs. To overcome the challenge of concentrating enough sunlight into such a tiny crystal, the team from MPL addressed the problem by designing an all-glass solar concentrator. The cone-shaped system collects sunlight with a Fresnel lens approximately the size of a household window and channels that light into an optical fiber about as wide as a human hair, the concentrated sunlight can then be directed onto the tiny nonlinear crystal responsible for producing the entangled photons.

The results were promising, the observed quantum states in the photons were 94% similar to perfectly entangled photons in lab settings. The entangled photons did not need to be of the same wavelength or color as well as have the same directions, they were entangled in their polarization, giving evidence that very incoherent light sources can still produce quantum entanglement in a single property given all other properties are maximally incoherent. The correlations violate classical Bell's Inequalities and thus cannot sufficiently be explained by classical theories. This can be potentially revolutionary for technologies that rely on quantum systems and states.

The researchers are now working to get better results, focusing on improving brightness, achieving quantum entanglement using other methods like four-wave mixing and overall improving the quality of entanglement. A truly remarkable feat of experimental physics.

Positive Psychology practices may improve blood pressure and heart health in just 8-12 weeks

A collaborative narrative analysis published in May 2026 in the journal Cardiology Clinics including University of South Florida, Northwestern University Feinberg School of Medicine and Stanford Medicine and led by Rosalba Hernandez, PhD from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign reviewed results from 18 randomized controlled trials that tested positive psychology and mindfulness interventions designed to improve mental or physical health. The programs varied widely in how they were delivered. Some relied on structured phone sessions, journaling paired with brief check-ins, or digital tools such as apps and text messages. Others involved interactive in-person group meetings or hybrid approaches that combined face-to-face activities with online tools and virtual sessions. Most programs included weekly sessions along with activities participants completed at home to reinforce what they had learned. The majority lasted between 6 and 12 weeks. The studies generally enrolled groups of 50-200 adults who already had elevated cardiovascular risk, including uncontrolled hypertension, heart failure, or other health conditions. Participants were usually in their late 50s to mid-60s. Among studies that reported gender, women made up 35-55% of the participants.

Previous studies had established correlations between mindfulness practices and cardiovascular health. This study established the strongest behavioral changes came from an eight-week WhatsApp-based program that paired weekly sessions with daily microtasks. Participants were encouraged to become more physically active, improve their diets, and take their medications as prescribed. Another program that incorporated motivational interviewing increased cardiac patients' activity by 1,800 steps per day while also improving medication adherence. By comparison, the mindfulness programs improved physical activity and diet, but did not produce the same medication adherence benefit, according to the study. "The therapeutic dose that was most consistently linked with improvements in blood pressure, inflammation and endothelial function was daily practice reinforced by weekly sessions over eight to 12-week periods," Hernandez said. "Therapeutic dosing typically involved high-frequency dosing over this time period to obtain short-term physiologic benefits, while ongoing less-intensive contact may be needed to sustain behavioral change."

While an optimal dosage has yet to be established, the review identified 8-12 weeks of frequent reinforcements by weekly sessions as the methods that worked best to maintain physical activity, diet and adherence to medication in the short term; long term cardiovascular health still needs to be monitored as effects may be acute, the study does acknowledge continued reinforcements to maintain such benefits. Further studies must also be conducted in healthier populations and individuals of varying ages for such results to be conclusive. Regardless, it is a step in the right direction, and can lead to better focus on psychological care followed by episodes of cardiac incidents that can lead to more holistic and faster recovery.
